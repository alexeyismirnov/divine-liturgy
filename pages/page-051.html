As Taft explains: St Germanus (†A.D. 730) integrated another level of interpretation into Byzantine liturgical understanding; one that was equally rooted in the New Testament and also found, though far less prominently, in Maximus (†A.D. 630) and other earlier Byzantine liturgical writings. This was the interpretation of the Eucharist, not only as the anamnesis* [recalling] of, but also as the actual figure of salvation history in Jesus. Each part of the service was linked with particular events in the life, death, burial, and resurrection of Christ.

The transfer of the gifts from the Prothesis to the Holy Altar and the singing of the Cherubim Hymn are symbolic of Jesus Christ’s journey from Bethany to Jerusalem to endure His suffering and death. When He entered Jerusalem there were many people, including children, who offered songs in a visible way to Jesus as king and as one who triumphs over death. In a similar but spiritual way, the angels continually offer songs to the thrice-Holy Trinity and people now lift up the Cherubim Hymn to God.

The placing of the Holy Gifts on the Altar represents the crucifixion of Christ, taking down His body from the cross, and His burial. This is seen in the prayers (troparion*) which are said when the Gifts are placed on the Holy Table. The Diskos* [paten] symbolizes the hands of Joseph and Nicodemus who took down the Immaculate Body of Christ from the cross, wrapped Him in clean burial cloths, and placed Him in the new tomb. The Chalice symbolizes the cup with the blood which flowed from Christ’s side, hands, and feet. The covering on the Diskos represents the cloth which was placed around His head, the covering on the Chalice represents the cloth in which His body was wrapped, and the aer represents the stone which Joseph placed on the entrance to the tomb. The incense represents the spices which were placed on the body of the Savior; and the Holy Altar and the Antimension represent the grave itself. Finally, the closed Holy Doors and the drawn curtain represent the door to the tomb which was securely closed by the soldiers and the seal. The Chalice is placed to the right of the diskos since the blood and water flowed from the right rib of the Savior.
