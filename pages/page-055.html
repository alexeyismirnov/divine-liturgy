Prayer of the Prothesis

During this litany the priest prays: ‘O Lord God Almighty…’ This prayer is called the Prayer of Offering (or Prothesis) since the offering is repeated several times during the liturgy, three times in fact. During the first offering – at the Prothesis – the priest prays that the Lord God would bless the gifts which are offered…. During the second offering – which is done now – the priest prays that the Lord would enable him to offer the gifts and spiritual sacrifices to Him, make us worthy to find grace before Him, that our sacrifice would be acceptable, and that the good Spirit of God’s grace would rest on the clergy, on the gifts which have been placed on the altar and on the people. These two offerings are only preparations for sacrifice…. With the third offering, which is the greatest and fullest, after remembering Christ’s suffering and counting God’s good works, the priest proclaims, ‘Thine own of Thine own we offer unto Thee in behalf of all and for all,’ after which comes the actual sanctification of the Divine Gifts.

Question

The priest prays that God’s grace would be on him, the gifts, and all the people. What is the relationship between God’s grace and our good works?

Litany of Supplication

The “Angel of Peace” litany, or the αἰτήσεις (asking), is an ancient prayer said at various times in a variety of church services. In the Apostolic Constitutions (c. 380 A.D.) it is used as a concluding litany before the dismissal of the faithful at matins and vespers. St John Chrysostom refers to the prayer in his Homily 3 on the epistle to the Colossians: “For each believer has an Angel; since even from the beginning, every one of those that were approved had his Angel…. If then we have Angels, let us be sober, as though we were in the presence of tutors; for there is a demon present also. Therefore we pray, asking for the Angel of peace, and everywhere we ask for peace (for there is nothing equal to this); peace.”
