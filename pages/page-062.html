The Anaphora — “The Offering Up”

The Preparation

Deacon: Let us stand aright! Let us stand with fear! Let us attend, that we may offer the Holy Oblation* in peace.

People: A mercy of peace! A sacrifice of praise!

Priest: The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God the Father, and the communion of the Holy Spirit be with all of you.

People: And with your spirit.

Priest: Let us lift up our hearts.

People: We lift them up unto the Lord.

The priest takes the aer from off the Holy Things, kisses it, and lays it to one side.

The deacon makes a reverence, goes into the sanctuary and, taking a fan (rhipidia), devoutly fans the Holy Things. If there are no fans, he does this with one of the veils.

The Preparation

‘A mercy of peace, a sacrifice of praise’ signifies that the Holy Sacrifice is the greatest mercy to us from God, who made peace with mankind through the sacrifice of His only Son on the cross.

Let us stand aright…’ According to St John Chrysostom, this means that we stand as a man rightly stands before the Lord; that is, with fear and trembling and with an awakened and sober spirit. The choir answers with ‘A mercy of peace, a sacrifice of praise’ which signifies that the Holy Sacrifice is the greatest mercy to us from God, who made peace with mankind through the sacrifice of His only Son on the cross. It is also a Holy Sacrifice to God from us – a sacrifice of thanksgiving and praise to Him. But since we are not in ourselves capable of offering to God this most solemn sacrifice, the priest comes with a proclamation of God’s gifts. Turning to the west he blesses with the words of II Corinthians 13.14, ‘The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God the Father, and the communion of the Holy Spirit be with you all.’ This is an appeal for good things from each Person of the Holy Trinity: grace from the Son, love from the Father, and communion from the Holy Spirit.
