The Intercession

People: … And we pray unto Thee, Our God. (repeated from page 68)

Priest: ▶ That they may be to those who partake for the purification of soul, for the remission of sins, for the communion of Thy Holy Spirit, for the fulfillment of the Kingdom of Heaven, for boldness towards Thee, and not for judgment or condemnation. Again we offer unto Thee this reasonable worship for those who have fallen asleep in the faith: ancestors, fathers, patriarchs, prophets, apostles, preachers, evangelists, martyrs, confessors, ascetics, and every righteous spirit made perfect in faith. …

The priest makes a low bow before the altar and continues praying: ▶

The priest then exclaims out loud: ▶

Priest: ▶ … Especially for our most holy, most pure, most blessed and glorious Lady Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary. …

The Hymn to the Theotokos

(On certain Feast Days this is replaced by another hymn.)

People: It is truly meet to bless you, O Theotokos, ever-blessed and most pure, and the Mother of our God. More honorable than the Cherubim and more glorious beyond compare than the Seraphim: without defilement you gave birth to God the Word: true Theotokos, we magnify you.

Priest: … For the holy Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John; the holy glorious, and all laudable apostles; Saint (saint of the day) whom we commemorate today; and all Thy saints, at whose supplication look down upon us, O God. Remember all those who have fallen asleep before us in the hope of resurrection to eternal life, especially (he remembers by name whom he will of those who have fallen asleep); grant them rest, O God, where the light of Thy countenance shines on them.

The deacon censes the altar round about, and he remembers whom he will of the living and the dead.

Theotokos

“The title, Mother of God, took on a great importance in the fourth century, when a heretic named Nestorius – a man who held high office in the Church – claimed the one in Mary’s womb was certainly man, but that He was not God. Orthodox Christians, with one accord, said ‘Wrong!’ …for unless the One in Mary’s womb was and is God, we are dead in our sins. To safeguard the full deity of Christ, the Church has always insisted that Mary be rightly called – as Elizabeth discerned her to be (St Luke 1.43) – the Mother of God. This title, of course, does not mean mother of the Holy Trinity, for the Holy Trinity has no mother. Neither does it mean she originated the Person who is God the Son, for He exists before all time. It refers instead to Mary being the God-bearer (Theotokos in Greek), Mother of the eternal Son of God, who assumed full humanity in her womb.”14
