Symbolic Meaning of the Great Entrance

In the earliest celebration of the Eucharist, the transfer of the peoples’ gifts to the Altar was done without any particular ceremony. Upon arrival at the meeting, believers would give the deacon their gifts of bread and wine and at some point during the service the deacon would take these offerings and place them on the Altar. Between the fourth and the seventh centuries many parts of the Divine Liturgy were made more ceremonial, including this transfer of the gifts, which became known as the Great Entrance.
