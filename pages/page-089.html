Forgive Me

“When the deacon comes forward, he asks forgiveness from the priest. In the same way the people who want to take communion, bow to those present and say either out loud or silently, ‘Forgive me,’ and then all answer, ‘May God forgive you.’”24 “Communion is given, first, for the remission of sins and is, therefore, the sacrament of reconciliation achieved by Christ in His own Sacrifice and eternally granted to those who believe in Him….”25

Questions

Do you honestly believe you need forgiveness? If so, from whom?

If you think of yourself as a sinner, do you think of specific sins or think of your life in general?

Sinners, of whom I am first

“This is a faithful saying and worthy of all acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am chief.” 1 Timothy 1.15

Comparing his own righteousness with the righteousness of God, he shows it to be worth nothing.

“The Prayer Before Communion is the confession of faith of St Peter and St Paul…Paul wrote Timothy that he was the worst of all sinners which demonstrates great humility before God.”26 In his homily on 1 Timothy 1.15 St John Chrysostom asks: “But how is it, that he [Paul] here calls himself a sinner, no, the chief of sinners, whereas he elsewhere asserts that he was ‘as to the righteousness which is in the Law, found blameless?’ (Philippians 3.6) Because with respect to the righteousness which God has brought about (the justification which is really required) even those who are righteous in the law are sinners, ‘for all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God.’ (Romans 3.23)

Therefore he does not say righteousness simply, but ‘the righteousness which is in the Law.’ As a man that has acquired wealth, with respect to himself appears rich, but upon a comparison with the treasures of kings is very poor and the chief of the poor; so it is in this case. Compared with angels, even righteous men are sinners; and if Paul, who fulfilled the righteousness that is in the law, was the chief of sinners, what other man can be called righteous? For he says this not to condemn his own life as impure, let this not be imagined; but comparing his own legal righteousness with the righteousness of God, he shows it to be worth nothing, and not only so, but he proves those who possess it are sinners.”

Questions

When you think of great sinners, of whom do you think? Do you ever honestly think of yourself being among them?
