The Holy Kiss

The kiss was a sign of unity and fellowship from ancient times and is found throughout scripture. As a sign of unity, Paul tells the believers in Rome, Corinth, and Thessalonica all to ‘greet one another with a holy kiss’ (Rom 16.16, I Cor 16.20, II Cor 13.12, I Thess 5.26.) Peter also instructs believers to share the holy kiss. (I Peter 5.14) Jesus criticized Simon the Pharisee for not kissing Him when he invited Him to a meal. (St Luke 7.45) Judas’ treachery was made more hideous because he used the kiss of unity to betray his Master. (St Luke 22.48) Without any doubt, the kiss of peace was a vital part of the earliest celebrations of the Eucharist.

The Didache

The Didache, probably written during the last half of the first century, during the time the New Testament books were being written, states that Christians should: Assemble on the Lord’s Day, and break bread and offer the Eucharist; but first make confession of your faults, so that your sacrifice may be a pure one. Anyone who has a difference with his fellow is not to take part with you until they have been reconciled, so as to avoid any profanation of your sacrifice…. The early church was so concerned that there be no divisions between the participants in the Eucharist that the bishops and presbyters held what amounted to church court early in the week so that disputes could be settled by Sunday. The Syrian Didascalia of the Apostles (probably written before 250 A.D.) states that let your judgments be held on the second day of the week, that if perchance any one should contest the sentence of your words, you may have space until the Sabbath to compose the matter, and may make peace between them on the Sunday.
