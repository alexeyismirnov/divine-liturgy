The Remembrance

The eucharistic prayer becomes now remembrance of Christ – of His coming to us and of the fulfillment in Him of the whole purpose of salvation.

The eucharistic prayer, after it has resounded with the glorious sound of the Sanctus, becomes now remembrance (anamnesis*) of Christ – of His coming to us (…Who when He had come…) and of the fulfillment in Him of the whole purpose of salvation (…and had fulfilled all the dispensation for us …). His Life, His Death, His Resurrection, one movement of sacrifice, of love, of dedication to His Father and to men – this is of the inexhaustible content of our remembrance. All this is our Eucharist which we “show forth” before God, which we “remember” before Him.

And then we come to the last night, to Christ’s last supper with those “whom He loved unto the end.” On that night in which He was given up…, He set up the Act, the Rite, the Sign, by which His unique and all-including Eucharist — His own perfect Life, His own perfect Sacrifice, His own perfect Victory — would eternally be given to us, become ours, as our life in Him.
