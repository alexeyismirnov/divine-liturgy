The Offertory (continued)

Thus in the procession of Offertory our very life is being brought to the Altar, presented to God in an act of love and adoration. True it is the ‘King of Kings and Lord of Lords who comes to be sacrificed’ (hymn of Offertory, Great Saturday), it is His Entrance as Priest and Oblation*; and yet, in Him and with Him, we also lay on the paten*, the members of His Body, the partakers of His Humanity. ‘Let us put aside all earthly cares,’ sings the choir; and, indeed, have not all our cares been assumed in this unique and ultimate Care that transfigures all our life – in this movement of love which brings us to the Source and the Giver and the Content of Life?

The Cherubim Hymn

This hymn calls those present Christians, mystically representing the Cherubim (that is, comparing in some mystical way to the Cherubim and singing together with them the thrice-holy hymn to the Life-Creating Trinity) to lay aside all worldly cares. In other words, not thinking about property, glory, or pleasure but to worthily receive the King of all earthly and heavenly creatures with the honor paid to Him by the heavenly angelic hosts singing ‘Alleluia!’

“The Cherubim Hymn reminds us that from this moment until the end of the Liturgy we must be vigilant and must lay aside all earthly cares since, during communion, all will receive Christ, the Great King.”

St Germanus (†A.D. 730)

This hymn began to be used in church services during the time of Emperor Justinian II (565-578). It was introduced into the Liturgy in order that during the time church officials brought the Gifts to the Altar from where they had been prepared, the faithful, by listening to this hymn, would be filled with the most holy thoughts and would be prepared to worthily receive the King of all.
