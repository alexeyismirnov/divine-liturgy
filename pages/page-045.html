The Offertory

The Offertory is the sacrificial act of the Church offering to God the oblation of our lives.

“The Cherubim Hymn, the censing of the Altar and congregation, and the transfer of the eucharistic elements to the Altar (Great Entrance) constitute the first essential ‘movement’ of the Eucharist: the Offertory, which is the sacrificial act of the Church offering to God the oblation of our lives. We speak very often of the sacrifice of Christ, but so easily forget that the sacrifice of Christ requests and implies our own sacrifice, or rather our ‘uniting ourselves’ to the sacrifice of Christ, for we are His Body and partakers of His Life. A sacrifice is the natural movement of love, which is the gift of oneself, a self-denial for the sake of the Other. When I love somebody, my life is in him whom I love; I give him my life – freely, willingly, joyfully – and this ‘giving away’ becomes the very meaning of my life.”1

Prayers of the Faithful

“As the first part of the Liturgy of the Word begins with a call to the believers to pray in peace to the Lord, so the Liturgy of the Faithful also calls for those present to pray in peace to which the choir responds ‘Lord have mercy.’ During this time, the priest recites the First Prayer of the Faithful in which he prays for forgiveness of his own sins and of the peoples’ and also prays for help in offering the bloodless sacrifice. The deacon then begins a second prayer during which time the priest says the Second Prayer of the Faithful where he prays for the cleansing of the bodies and souls of those who stand before the Altar, for progress in life, faith, and spiritual understanding of those who pray, and that the taking of communion might be done worthily…. At the end of both these prayers the deacon proclaims ‘Wisdom’ which reminds those present that their attention during the offering of the gifts for sacrifice on the Altar must be focused and they should not be distracted since the consecration of the gifts is done with particular care.”2
