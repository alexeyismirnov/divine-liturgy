Question

What comfort and challenge can be found in the fact that Christ continually makes intercession for us before God?

Therefore He [Jesus] is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them. Hebrews 7.25

It is Christ who died, and furthermore is also risen, who is even at the right hand of God, who also makes intercession for us. Romans 8.34

Prayer for the Church

A parish as a human institution can easily replace Christ with something else.

“Among the first, remember, O Lord… our Bishop….” When we pray for the Bishop, we pray for the real “welfare” of the Church, for her abiding in Divine Truth: that the Church may be the Church – the Presence of God, His Healing Power, His Love, His Truth – and not, as it so often happens, a selfish, self-centered community defending its own human ‘interest’ instead of the Divine purpose for which it exists. The Church so easily becomes an ‘institution,’ a ‘bureaucracy,’ a ‘money-raising’ group, a ‘nationalism,’ a ‘social gathering,’… How often people who “hunger and thirst after righteousness” do not see Christ in the Church, but see in it merely human pride and arrogance, selfishness and the spirit of ‘this world.’ Of all this the Eucharist is judgment and condemnation. …it is not mere belonging to the Church that saves, for there is no magic in Christianity, but the acceptance of the Spirit of Christ. And this spirit judges not only individuals but also congregations, parishes, diocese. A parish as a human institution can easily replace Christ with something else: the worldly spirit of success, of human pride and human ‘achievements.’ The temptation is always present and ‘tempting.’ But then, the one whose sacred duty is always to minister the ‘Word of Truth,’ must remind the parish that it is being tempted, must condemn, in the name of Christ, all that is not compatible with the Spirit of Christ. It is for this courage and wisdom, love and faith fullness of the clergy, that we pray in the Prayer of Intercession.”19

Questions

What are our responsibilities towards our leaders? Why is it often easier to criticize them than to pray for them?

Obey those who rule over you, and be submissive, for they watch out for your souls, as those who must give account. Let them do so with joy and not with grief, for that would be unprofitable for you. Pray for us. Hebrews 13.17-18

Brethren, pray for us. 1 Thessalonians 5.25;  …and for me, that utterance may be given to me. Ephesians 6.19;  …meanwhile praying also for us, that God would open to us a door for the word. Colossians 4.3

“And grant that with one mouth and one heart we may praise Thine all honorable and majestic name:…one mouth, one heart, one redeemed humanity restored in love and knowledge of God – such is the end, the goal of the Liturgy, the fruit of the Eucharist. And the mercies of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ shall be with all of you. This is the achievement of the ‘second movement,’ that of God giving Himself to us, in His mercy, which passes all human understanding. The eucharistic prayer is over, and we come now to the fulfillment of everything the Eucharist has revealed: to the Communion, i.e. our partaking of its reality.”20
